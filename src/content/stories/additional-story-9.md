---
title: "9. From a Hundred Thousand to a Small Remnant"
slug: "additional-story-9"
---

<p><em>Source: Albert</em></p><p>Albert places the decline of Tunisia's Jewish population within a sequence of departures that unfolded over several periods. He recalls that Tunisia once had around one hundred thousand Jews, while the community today is only a small fraction of that number. He identifies independence and the events surrounding the Bizerte conflict as important periods, and he also describes a significant departure in 1967.</p><p>According to his account, attacks on Jewish homes and businesses during the events of 1967 frightened many families. Some left for France, while others went to Israel. He describes the departures as occurring in stages rather than as one single movement. Different families left at different moments and for different reasons.</p><p>The consequences were not only numerical. When families left, the places where they had lived, worked, prayed, and gathered changed as well. A smaller population meant fewer businesses, fewer social gatherings, and greater difficulty maintaining the communal routines that had once been familiar.</p><p>Albert names several places where Jewish communities had existed, including Tunis, Sfax, Kef, Djerba, Bizerte, Nabeul, and Sousse. He also distinguishes between regions: some communities lived in identifiable Jewish quarters, while in other towns people lived alongside their neighbours within the same urban environment.</p><p>His account presents demographic decline as a transformation of an entire social landscape. The departure of families affected not only the number of people remaining, but also the everyday structures through which a community had once experienced its life.</p>
