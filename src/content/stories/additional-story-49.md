---
title: "The Principal Who Defended My Right to Say No"
slug: "additional-story-49"
---

One of the most difficult experiences I remember from public school involved a lesson in Islamic education.

As a Jewish student in a predominantly Muslim educational environment, I sometimes found myself in situations where my religious identity became impossible to ignore. During one lesson, the teacher repeatedly singled me out and instructed me to read the Quran aloud.

I felt uncomfortable. My discomfort was not a rejection of Islam or a lack of respect for my Muslim teachers. It came from being placed in a religious situation that conflicted with my own beliefs and in which I did not feel comfortable participating.

When the teacher called on me, I told her that I could not read it. She insisted that I had to. I tried to respond respectfully. I told her, in effect, that I respected her very much, but I could not do what she was asking me to do. Rather than accepting my answer, she called the principal.

I did not know what to expect. Being summoned to the principal's office could easily have made an already uncomfortable situation worse. But what happened next is something I have never forgotten.

The principal, Mr. Hamudi, took my side.

He made it clear that I did not have to read the Quran. He defended my right to decline and recognized that my religious identity mattered. He understood that respect for a teacher did not mean I had to violate my own convictions to demonstrate it.

I remember him with enormous affection. He was one of the kindest people I knew, a gentle and soft-hearted man who treated me with compassion when I needed it.

What made his intervention so meaningful was that he could have simply sided with the teacher because of her authority. Instead, he listened to the situation and stood up for me.

I have always believed that teachers occupy a highly respected place in society. Even now, it pains me to describe situations in which educators treated me unfairly. But remembering Mr. Hamudi reminds me that authority and compassion do not have to be opposites.

He simply recognized that I had a right to say no. Of all the things I remember from school, his kindness remains one of the clearest examples of what education can be when it is guided by humanity as well as authority.
