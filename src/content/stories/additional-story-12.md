title: "12. Ten People and the Fragility of a Minyan"

<p><em>Source: Albert</em></p><p>Albert explains that certain Jewish prayers require a minyan, a quorum of ten people. Without the required number, the service cannot proceed in the usual way. His explanation is brief, but it points to a practical consequence of demographic decline: when a community becomes very small, the presence of each individual can matter greatly.</p><p>A minyan is not simply a number on a page. It depends on people being available at the same time and willing or able to attend. In a larger community, gathering ten people may be relatively straightforward. In a much smaller one, arranging the quorum can become a challenge.</p><p>Albert's remarks place religious continuity within the realities of community size. The decline in population affects more than the number of families or the visibility of Jewish life in public. It can affect whether a particular prayer service can be held.</p><p>This is one reason the remaining relationships and institutions matter. A community needs people who continue to attend, participate, and help sustain its religious life. The importance of each member becomes more visible when there are fewer members overall.</p><p>Albert does not describe every logistical difficulty involved in arranging a minyan, so the account should not be expanded into specific scenes that he did not provide. What his answer does establish is the principle: the quorum is required, and the reduced size of the community makes the participation of its members especially consequential.</p>
