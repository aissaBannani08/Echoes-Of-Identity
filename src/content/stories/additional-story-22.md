<h2>Story 1. Rahaf: A Life Recast by War</h2><p><em>Themes: motherhood · displacement · daily survival · children's hopes</em></p><p>My name is Rahaf. I am a mother of seven, and I am from Gaza. My family of nine lives in a tent, in circumstances that are very hard.</p><p>Most of my days go to keeping my children alive. I light fires and spend hours cooking food and heating water for them. Even wood costs us something we can barely spare, but there is no other way.</p><p>The tent is not a safe place. It is full of insects, fleas, cockroaches and rats. At night I am afraid for my children, and I can’t always protect them from what crawls around them.</p><p>Still, my children keep dreaming. One day I asked them to draw what they wished for. They drew a home, safety, security, peace and freedom, the things the war took from them. When I look at those drawings, I see how much they have lost, and how much they still hope for.</p><p>My youngest son is not yet three. He was born during the war, so he has never known anything else. Recently he finally got a toy, and I watched his face light up. It was the first real joy I had seen in him in a long time. I wish a child’s happiness didn’t have to be so rare.</p><p>Another of my sons is quiet and patient far beyond his years. There is a story behind his smile that he doesn’t tell. He doesn’t ask for much, only a chance to live in peace.</p><p>So I ask people to stand with us and not to leave my children alone. They are suffering in these tents, and we need help.</p><p>But I also want you to know that we are still here, still hoping. For their sake, we endure and dream of a brighter day. My daughter’s name is Wateen Al-Rouh.</p><p>I had always dreamed of building a family. But before I could begin that life, I lost my husband while I was still pregnant with our daughter. I was six months pregnant, and the grief took a heavy toll on me. I gave birth in a hospital in southern Gaza as explosions sounded nearby and fear filled the air. I held my newborn daughter, knowing that her father would never have the chance to meet her.</p><p>Just one day after giving birth, I had to leave the hospital because it was no longer safe. I carried my newborn back to a tent. She was only one day old. Since then, our suffering has continued to grow. The heat is unbearable, insects are everywhere, smoke from open fires fills the air, and hunger is a constant presence in our lives.</p><p>Providing even the most basic necessities for my daughter, including milk and diapers, is a daily struggle. It was the place where we felt safe, where our lives unfolded, and where we kept the things that mattered to us. Today, it lies in ruins. Since then, every day has been a struggle.</p><p>Yet, I keep reminding myself that the war has taken so much from us, but it has not extinguished the roots of hope in my heart. They are still alive, even among the rubble.</p><p>As we try to rebuild our lives, I am raising donations to help my family endure these difficult circumstances. We urgently need basic necessities, and every contribution brings us a little closer to meeting those needs and facing the days ahead.</p><p>Even in the midst of so much hardship, the children around me remind me why we must keep going. Their strength amazes me, and seeing a child smile can bring hope back into my day. Life remains uncertain, and each day brings new challenges, but we continue to hold on to one another and find reasons to keep moving forward.</p><p>I want people to see what has happened to my home and my city, but also to remember the people who live here. We are more than the destruction surrounding us. We are people with memories, families, dreams, and plans for the future. Despite everything we have lost, we still hope to rebuild our lives and find a measure of stability again.</p><p>Every act of support, no matter how small, can make a real difference to my family. Your kindness can help us meet our immediate needs and hold on to the hope that, one day, we will be able to build a safer and more secure future.</p><p>Regarding who I am, my name is Waseem and I am from Gaza. I stopped counting the places a long time ago and started counting what we carried instead, which was less each time. It’s safety, the simple feeling of closing my eyes and trusting that the night will pass quietly. I miss food that arrives without a struggle.
